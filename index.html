A data story about television since 1990

Television Got Shorter

Oscar Wong
October 2026
Data from The Movie Database and Netflix

Every country that makes television has cut the length of its seasons. The places that ran longest fell furthest, and most of the world now makes seasons of 6 to 12 episodes, close to the British habit of six.

28 of 28 countries with enough data made shorter seasons after 2015 than before 2005
6.0 episodes in a typical British season, in the 1990s, 2000s, 2010s and 2020s alike
85 to 10 episodes in a typical Mexican season, before 2005 and since 2015

Reading the colours

  • Orange is always the United Kingdom.
  • Blue is always season length, in episodes.
  • Green is always a share of new series.
  • Plum is always Malaysia.
  • Grey is context: other countries for comparison, or too little data to measure.

01

Long seasons were a world habit

Before 2005, how long a season ran depended on where it was made. A typical Mexican series ran 85 episodes a season, an Indian one 42 and a Taiwanese one 40. A typical British series ran six.

“Typical” here means the median. Line up a country’s series from the shortest season to the longest and take the one in the middle. A few soap operas with hundreds of episodes would drag an average upward, but they barely move the median.

Why some countries are grey

Grey does not mean a country makes no television. It means The Movie Database, the volunteer-built catalogue behind these numbers, lists fewer than 10 of its series for that period. That is true of most of Africa, Southeast Asia and the Middle East, and of Malaysia, which has no listed series from before 2005 and 8 since 2015. Older figures also rest on smaller samples: Mexico’s 85 comes from 43 series, against 1,389 for the United States. Hong Kong qualifies but is too small to draw at this scale.

Since 2015 the map has paled. Every country shortened its seasons, and the countries that ran longest cut the most. Of the 27 countries shown, 25 now sit between 6 and 12 episodes. China, at 30, and Türkiye, at 15, are the exceptions, and both still fell.

The cartogram below puts before and after in one picture. Each country becomes a circle, placed near its spot on the map and nudged so no two overlap. The outer ring is its old season length and the filled circle is its new one, so the gap between them is how much it cut. The United Kingdom is the one circle that is nearly solid, because its seasons were already short.

02

The long season faded year by year

The maps compare two snapshots. The lines below fill in the years between. In every large producer the line drifts down towards the British one, which barely moves. American series went from a median of 17.5 episodes a season in the 1990s to 8 in the 2020s.

China is the clear exception. Its dramas still run about 30 episodes a season, though that is down from more than 40.

Pooling every country shows what replaced the long season. In 1990, one new series in eight ran 21 to 26 episodes, the length of a full American network season. By 2025 it was one in 25.

The 7 to 10 episode season has been the most common length every year since 2015, and the British six now makes up one new series in five.

More series, more recently

The Movie Database lists far more recent series than old ones. Each year in the 1990s rests on 160 to 294 series, and each year since 2020 on 1,413 to 1,937. That is why these charts use shares and medians rather than counts.

The United States shows the change most clearly. In the 1990s its new series bunched at 13 and 22 episodes, the half and full seasons that American networks ordered. By the 2020s the peaks had moved to 6, 8 and 10, on top of the British shape.

Newcomers and old hands

More countries reach 10 series in recent periods, from 17 in the late 1990s to 47 today, and most newcomers make short seasons. But the narrowing is not only about newcomers: every one of the 28 countries measured in both eras shortened its seasons.

Countries did not only shorten their seasons. They came together. From 2000 to 2004, the middle half of countries ran between 13 and 28 episodes a season. From 2020 to 2025 they ran between 6.5 and 10. The United Kingdom sat at six throughout, and the rest of the world moved towards it.

03

Fewer launches in autumn, fewer second chances

The season shrank in another sense too. American networks used to launch their new shows together each September. In the 1990s, 38% of new American series premiered in that one month. In the 2020s, 10% did, about the same as most other months.

What counts as renewed

A series counts as renewed when The Movie Database lists two or more seasons for it. Miniseries are left out, because they are made to end after one season and their share of new series has grown.

Fewer series now get to continue. Of the series that premiered in 2010, 57 in every 100 came back for a second season. Of those from 2022, 36 did.

The 2022 group has had the least time, so a few more may still return. But the 2019 group has had six years and sits at 45.

04

What Malaysia watches

So far this is a story about what gets made. Netflix’s weekly Top 10 for Malaysia shows what people here actually watch. Between July 2021 and October 2026, 635 series made the list, and 624 of them were matched to The Movie Database to find where each was made and how long its seasons run.

South Korea leads by far. Korean series filled 47% of all the weeks in Malaysia’s Top 10, nearly three times the American share. Series made in Malaysia filled 8%.

About the Netflix data

Netflix publishes a Top 10 of series for each country every week. Each week a show spends on the list counts once, whatever its rank. Eleven series could not be matched and are left out; they account for 15 of 2,750 weeks. On the map, a series made by several countries is split evenly between them.

Malaysian series came fourth overall, led by the animated BoBoiBoy Galaxy, Mechamato and Upin & Ipin. They have climbed, from last place in 2021, behind even British series, to fourth in every year since 2024, behind only Korea, the United States and Japan.

Reading the rows

Each series sits in the row of the first country The Movie Database lists for it. The database records some long anime as a single season, which is why a few circles reach past 40.

The series Malaysians choose are short too, if not as short as British ones. Korean and Japanese series in the Top 10 have a median of 12 episodes a season, Malaysian ones 10 and American ones 8. British series, at six, are the shortest. Eleven pass 40 episodes a season, most of them anime and children’s shows.

05

The odd one out became the norm

In 1990 the six-episode British series looked strange next to a 22-episode American season or a Mexican drama that ran for months. Thirty-five years later, most of the world makes seasons of 6 to 12 episodes, and the shows Malaysians watch most sit in the same range.

Viewers get shorter stories and fewer of them come back. The British habit of making a few episodes, then stopping, is now how most television is made.

Data

Series, seasons and episodes: The Movie Database (TMDB) API, retrieved October 2026. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.

What Malaysia watches: Netflix Top 10, weekly lists of series for Malaysia from 4 July 2021 to 4 October 2026, retrieved October 2026.

Country outlines: Natural Earth, via the world-atlas package.

Method

Episodes per season is a series’ episode count divided by its season count. Series first aired from 1990 to 2025 are used. Series averaging more than 100 episodes a season, such as daily soaps, talk shows and news, are left out; they are the longest and mostly old, so leaving them out makes the fall look smaller, not larger.

Each country’s figure is the median across the series it made, and a co-production counts for every country involved. A country is shown only when TMDB lists at least 10 of its series in that period. Netflix titles were matched to TMDB by name and air date.
